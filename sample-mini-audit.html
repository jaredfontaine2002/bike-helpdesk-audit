Finding 2 — One macro that does not fit

The macro, the failure, and the fix

Existing macro (invented for this sample) — “Cassette swap — yes it fits”:

“Yes — an 11-speed cassette will work on your bike. Go ahead and order, and let us know if you need anything else.”

Why it fails: it answers before it knows. An 11-speed cassette needs the right freehub driver body; on a wheel built for 8/9/10-speed Shimano HG, it does not fit without a wheel or driver change. In the synthetic set, 14 tickets used this macro; 5 came back as returns or follow-up complaints. A macro that sounds helpful and is sometimes wrong is worse than no macro.

Revised macro — asks first, escalates on condition:

“Before I confirm: which wheel and hub is the cassette going on, and do you know the driver body (for example Shimano HG, Micro Spline, or SRAM XD)? If you can add a photo of the driver, I can confirm the same day.
Escalation: if the driver body is unknown, or the wheel is not one we stock, do not confirm compatibility — draft the reply for a mechanic to check against the catalogue first.”

The revised version is a draft the agent or a staff member sends only after the facts exist. Compatibility verdicts stay draft-only until a human checks them — that rule does not change with automation.

Finding 3 — One routing issue

Warranty tickets sitting in the general queue

In the synthetic set, warranty tickets waited a median of 31 hours for a first reply (invented figure, shown to demonstrate the format) against 4 hours for order status. The cause in this sample: the routing rule only fires on the exact phrase “warranty claim”. Customers write “my frame cracked”, “it snapped”, “broken after two rides” — none of which match, so the tickets land in the general inbox behind sizing questions. The fix is a wider intent rule plus a triage macro that collects serial number, proof of purchase and photos in one pass, so the first human touch is a decision, not a request for attachments.

Prioritised recommendation

  1. Fix the compatibility macro first (above). It is the only finding in this sample that can actively cost the shop money on a single ticket.
  2. Widen the warranty routing rule and install the one-pass evidence macro. Cheapest change in the list; removes the slowest lane's main cause.
  3. Deflect order status with order data — 19% of this sample queue is answerable without a human once the reply pulls live order status.

One thing this Mini-Audit would NOT do yet

It would not automate warranty replies. Warranty outcomes are draft-only, forever — no setup built from this audit sends, refunds or promises a warranty result without a human approving it. Automating the evidence collection is safe; automating the decision is not, and the sample does not recommend it.
